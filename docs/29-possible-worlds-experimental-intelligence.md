# ATLAS 1.9 — Possible Worlds & Experimental Intelligence

## Core change

ATLAS no longer stores hypotheses as passive statements.

It builds explicit possible worlds:

W_i = assumptions + priors + predicted observations + source provenance.

It then asks which feasible experiment most efficiently separates the worlds.

## Experimental objective

The default objective combines:

- expected information gain;
- pairwise discrimination power;
- feasibility;
- representativeness;
- robustness to model misspecification;
- cost;
- time;
- risk;
- reversibility.

This follows the logic of Bayesian optimal experimental design while adding ATLAS governance constraints.

## Minimal discriminant experiment

A good experiment is not necessarily the experiment with the strongest signal.

ATLAS prefers the smallest safe experiment that produces enough information to distinguish competing worlds.

This prevents expensive or dangerous experiments when a cheap discriminant exists.

## Identifiability

If two worlds predict almost the same observable distribution, ATLAS marks them as currently indistinguishable.

No amount of rhetoric promotes one over the other.

The next task becomes:
find a new measurement or intervention whose predictions diverge.

## Reality Gap Monitor

After an experiment:

prior worlds -> observation -> Bayesian posterior -> reality-gap report.

Worlds that repeatedly assign very low probability to observed outcomes lose posterior weight.

Unexpected outcomes generate new research questions rather than being silently ignored.

## World Ensemble

ATLAS should avoid binary framing.

A research question may contain:
- conventional world;
- alternative mechanism A;
- alternative mechanism B;
- measurement-artifact world;
- adversarial/fraud/error world;
- unknown-mechanism residual world.

This greatly reduces false dichotomies.

## Experiment Economy

Experiment utility is measured as knowledge per unit of:

- money;
- time;
- risk;
- irreversibility;
- regulatory burden;
- opportunity cost.

## Robustness

Pure information seeking can fail when the model family is wrong.

ATLAS therefore keeps:
- representativeness;
- robustness;
- negative controls;
- out-of-distribution checks;
- explicit unknown-world residuals.

## Recommended additional advances

### Unknown World Reserve
Always reserve some probability mass for:
"none of the current models is adequate."

### Counterevidence Generator
Before testing a favorite hypothesis, generate evidence that would weaken it.

### Experiment Mutation
If worlds remain indistinguishable, automatically mutate:
- sensor;
- intervention;
- timing;
- context;
- population;
- reference frame.

### Discovery Dividend
A failed hypothesis may still produce:
- a new sensor;
- a better algorithm;
- a useful dataset;
- a negative result;
- a new causal variable.

ATLAS records those outputs separately from whether the hypothesis survived.

### Cross-Domain Mechanism Transfer
Mechanisms validated in one domain may be proposed in another only as new hypotheses, never copied as truth.

Example:
resonance in electrical systems
-> candidate synchronization model in physiology
-> new hypothesis
-> independent validation required.

## Principle

The purpose of a hypothesis is not to win.
The purpose is to generate a world that can lose to reality.
