# ATLAS — Sumerian & Mesopotamian Research Corpus

## Objective

Create a research-grade source layer for Sumerian and wider Mesopotamian material: tablets, inscriptions, king lists, administrative texts, literary compositions, rituals, lexical material and traditions concerning Anuna/Anunnaki.

The system may investigate conventional and unconventional hypotheses, but must never collapse them into the same evidential category.

## Core corpora

### CDLI — Cuneiform Digital Library Initiative
Use for object-level evidence:
- museum/accession identifiers;
- provenience;
- period;
- object type/material;
- photos/handcopies when available;
- transliterations;
- bibliography;
- stable P/Q identifiers.

### ETCSL — Electronic Text Corpus of Sumerian Literature
Use for:
- myths;
- divine narratives;
- royal praise;
- Sumerian King List;
- rulers of Lagash;
- Tummal history;
- Gudea cylinders;
- city laments;
- letters and letter-prayers;
- hymns/cult songs;
- proverbs and debate literature.

A composite ETCSL edition may combine multiple tablets/fragments; it is not automatically a single archaeological object.

### ETCSRI / RIME — Royal inscriptions
Use for:
- Presargonic rulers;
- Sargonic/Gutian periods;
- Gudea and Lagash;
- Ur III;
- building and dedicatory inscriptions;
- royal titulary;
- warfare and territorial claims;
- temple construction;
- ruler-deity relationships.

### ePSD2
Use for:
- lemma;
- spelling variants;
- period distribution;
- divine/proper names;
- corpus frequency.

### ORACC AMGG
Use as a scholarly interpretive index for deities and terminology. Primary-text claims must still be traced to corpus attestations.

### CAMS / other ORACC corpora
Use for later Akkadian/Babylonian/Assyrian learned texts, rituals, omens, magic, commentaries and later Anunnaki attestations.

## Anuna / Anunnaki protocol

Search normalized and written variants:
- Anuna
- Anunak
- Anunna
- Anunnaki
- Anunnaku
- Anunnakku
- dA.NUN
- dA.NUN.NA
- dA.NUN.NA.KE.E.NE
- d a-nun
- d a-nun-na
- d a-nun-na-ki

Do not assume these forms are identical in every period or genre without philological review.

For every occurrence capture:
1. corpus;
2. object/text ID;
3. date/period;
4. provenience;
5. genre;
6. line reference;
7. transliteration;
8. translation;
9. grammatical role;
10. surrounding divine names;
11. scholarly commentary;
12. alternative reading;
13. confidence.

## Evidence ladder

H0 — modern assertion only  
H1 — translation or secondary citation exists  
H2 — scholarly edition/transliteration identified  
H3 — exact cuneiform object/manuscript identified  
H4 — independent manuscripts or inscriptions converge  
H5 — archaeological/contextual evidence converges  
H6 — discriminant prediction or independently testable model

No claim becomes proved merely because a myth contains a motif.

## Ancient-astronaut / extraterrestrial hypotheses

ATLAS may investigate them, but stores them as hypotheses unless stronger evidence warrants change.

Required discriminants:
- original lexical meaning;
- whether the claimed word/phrase actually occurs;
- manuscript date;
- whether the translation is philologically accepted;
- whether alleged technical language is really present in the cuneiform;
- whether archaeology independently supports the interpretation;
- whether conventional explanations account for the same evidence.

Modern claims remain modern_interpretation or fringe_claim and are never promoted silently to primary evidence.

## Initial research questions

1. What are the earliest secure attestations of Anuna/Anunnaki?
2. How does the semantic/function profile change by period and genre?
3. Which Gudea passages contain Anuna references?
4. How do Sumerian and Akkadian uses differ?
5. What exactly does the Sumerian King List say, manuscript by manuscript?
6. Which kings are independently attested archaeologically and epigraphically?
7. Which antediluvian rulers are known only from literary/chronographic tradition?
8. Which modern Anunnaki claims can be traced to a specific cuneiform line?
9. Which claims fail because the alleged wording is absent from primary texts?
10. Which unresolved anomalies remain after philological and archaeological controls?
